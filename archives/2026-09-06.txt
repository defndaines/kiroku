## Fiction

This is my second book by Mira Grant, recommended by a BookTubers I like. In “Overgrowth”, Anastasia has grown up telling everyone who will listen that she is an alien, the harbinger of a coming invasion. She has a boyfriend and a couple close friends who “believe” her, unaware that she was swapped as a toddler by a seed pod. Now an alien broadcast has been released to the world, and Stasia must find out where she stands. I have to admit, I was nearly half way into the book before I started questioning whether humanity was a protagonist. Grant’s writing is propulsive, and I wasn’t quite expecting where this would go.


A friend told me about “The Stone Face” a while back. Simeon has self-exiled himself to Paris after a rough experience in Philadelphia. In the French capital, he is treated as any American and not discriminated against because he is black. But as he comes to understand the situation of the Algerians, he starts to question his own privilege that has allowed him this safe space. This is a bit of a lost classic, one of the first books to portray the police riot against Algerians (and banned in France as a result). It’s a smooth read while also being a deep introspection of black identity in the late ’50s and early ’60s. Highly recommended.

## Short Fiction

I’d queued up this short story by H.H. Pak before they became the youngest person to ever win a Hugo Award. “When You Give a Dokkaebi Taco Tuesday” imagines a dokkaebi inhabiting a broom and brought to America by the man whose family line they are bound to curse. But life is different here, and at night our dokkaebi gets out and meets other supernatural beings at the taco truck. A fun story playing with myth and the meaning of generational curses.

## Graphic Novels

I had read chapter 1 of “The City Beneath Her Feet” in 2024, and thought it wasn’t going to get completed, but luckily when chapter 3 came out I was also able to get a copy of chapter 2, which I’d missed. This continues the story of Zara in the aftermath of what happens to Jasper in the first book. It’s a world of secret assassins that she inadvertently becomes aware of. The story wraps up nicely and has an emotional appeal even if the set-up starts to get a bit cheesy.

“A Mischief of Magpies #1” follows Mar as he grows up, with an imagination that others don’t appreciate, partly because his parents have to deal with his younger sister’s disease. But periodically, Mar is thrust into a different world, and that world has a mission for him which will prove his worth. The magpies lead him to a floating city that is under attack. An interesting—if heady and artsy—start to the tale that I’m intrigued to follow.
