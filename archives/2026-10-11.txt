## Fiction

I’d heard about “The Curse of Hester Gardens” before Worldcon, and then saw Thompson on a couple panels. Nona has raised a family in Hester Gardens, in a Detroit suburb. But her husband has been in jail for years, and her eldest died at eighteen. Her second son is graduating valedictorian and heading to Brown, but there are paranormal forces at work which seem to not want him to escape. The blend of horror with social reality gives this book its impact. We see what it takes to stay afloat, along with all the forces keeping folks down. Recommended, and queue up “Soledad Brother” before or after you read.

## Short Fiction

“In This Exchange of Names, I Say Please” is a flash fiction story looking back on growing up Chinese-American, immigrating as a child; the burden to assimilate and the resentment of being expected to tend to other immigrants who have yet to learn English. A powerful short reflection, it’s my third story by the author, who I plan to keep track of.

I don’t remember how I heard of “A Heap of Petrified Gods”, but I’m glad I did. Just a few pages, this story follows a young man as he leaves Lagos for the metropole, taking his god with him. The story is perhaps a bit on the nose, but I still quite liked the framing and sense of what the dominant countries expect in order to assimilate. A speculative tale I definitely recommend.
