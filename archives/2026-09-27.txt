## Fiction

“Hello Baby” tells the story of several women in a shared support group as they go through IVF. Everyone is surprised when one of them announces their new baby a year after stopping treatment. With the framework of each woman’s experience trying to get pregnant, we see Korean society laid bare, and a fairly clear demonstration of why Korea has the lowest birthrate in the world. (And I was floored to learn that IVF in Korea is about 20–25% the cost compared to the U.S.) I recommend this easily, and it should be require reading for any man involved in the IVF process.

“Teo’s Durumi” is the conclusion of “Ocean’s Godori”, which I read over two years earlier. We gain a better understanding of the forces arrayed against Teo, and the team prepares to take on the villain. This is fun space opera, and a good conclusion to the story. It did feel a little slow and disconnected for me in the first half, and becomes action heavy at the end. Worth reading if you read the first book and liked it.

## Nonfiction

“Not Too Late” is a collection of essays I read because it is a book club selection. Published in 2023, some of it can feel a little dated already, as many essays are still excited about the Green New Deal and the Inflation Reduction Act (that included more than a trillion dollars in subsidies to climate-polluting companies). But the overall message is one of hope, and how to derive inspiration to keep fighting. On several occasions I was reminded of Samwise’s point of fighting because it’s the right thing to do. Included are essays by people already on the receiving end of climate disaster as well as by experts in the scientific and political arenas. A quick read, it isn’t bad, but also isn’t a recommendation.

## Graphic Novels

“Kya and the Secret of the Sand” is a story about Kya finding herself in the world, framed as the aunt recounting this for Korra as she struggle with recovery after Zaheer. Setting out on her own for the first time, Kya journeys to the Si Wong Desert to offer her services as a healer. The locals are struggling, and their water supplies seem to be getting lower and lower. This is a fun addition to the universe, so an easy recommendation for fans of the Korra series.
