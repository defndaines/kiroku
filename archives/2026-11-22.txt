## Nonfiction

I’d been meaning to read “Walkable City” for some time, and it was this month’s selection for the Urban book club. The book is over fourteen years old, so I’ve managed to pick up a lot of the information here over the years since it’s had such an influence on other media I track. Most of it still seems pretty solid, though from time to time I did wonder if some recommendations would change a bit because of new data. But at its core, this is still essential reading for anyone seeking a grounding in contemporary urbanism.

I’d been looking for potential book club recommendations about A.I., and “Obsolete” bubbled up as the most promising. This is a great primer for A.I., the companies building it today, and what we can expect from it in the future. I felt like anyone with barely a passing knowledge of the subject would learn a lot. The book feels rather balanced, taking on different arguments that are being made, from “Super Intelligence” hopefuls to doomers and across the spectrum. He makes a solid argument that this is something everyone has a stake in, and provides reasonable action items, even if I don’t 100% agree with them. If you are reading one book on A.I., it should be this.
