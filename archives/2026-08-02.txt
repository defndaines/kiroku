## Fiction

From the description, “Dominion” didn’t seem quite my jam, but a BookTuber I trust gave it a strong recommendation after it was shortlisted for the Women’s Prize. In the fictional town of Dominion, the Winfrey family is headed by patriarch Sabre, who is also pastor at a highly successful Baptist church. But under the surface, things are quite different. Matriarch Priscilla has just about had enough. And youngest, “Wonderboy”, is in a passionate teenage relationship with Diamond, but is it mutual? Told from different perspectives, the story builds up slowly and in ways I wasn’t expecting. Recommended.

I’d been meaning to read “Martyr!” but couldn’t get it in time for book club, so it went into the backlog. Cyrus was born in Iran, but after the U.S. murders his mother (Iran Air Flight 655), infant Cyrus and his father migrate to Indiana. Now an orphan in his late twenties, the recovering alcoholic is working on a book about martyrs. A friend tells him about an Iranian artist, Orkideh, running her last artwork: sitting in an NYC museum and conversing with strangers as she dies from breast cancer. If asked to summarize this book a third of the way in, I would have struggled. But this comes together beautifully, and the author is as much a poet as his protagonist. Definitely recommended.

## Short Fiction

I learned of “¡Sangronas!” because it was an Ignyte Award finalist last year. A second-person narrative set in a near future Hayward, California; it is partly structured around a running “list of rules”. A new initiate is learning the ropes, how to stay safe and how to survive in a world hostile to their kind. Purposefully confusing in its telling, you are never 100% certain what the sangronas are capable of. I liked the way this could be read multiple ways. Recommended.

## Nonfiction

I’m not sure where I heard about “Emplumada”, probably a trusted recommendation since it’s been on my shelf for a while. This landmark poetry collection was Cervantes’ first publication. She pulls on her personal history, and peppers the poems with Spanish. Topics include poverty, abuse, racism, and being a woman. The cadence encourages you to read aloud, or at least in an “aloud” voice in your head. I found myself dwelling on the imagery evoked. An excellent collection.
