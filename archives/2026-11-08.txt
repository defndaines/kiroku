## Fiction

I put in a hold for “The Rouse” when I saw Miéville had a new book coming out, 1250 pages be damned. We see Maur as she raises her son as a single mother; and we see him slip away, drawn to violence and the military despite her best efforts. When he passes, her grief leads her to investigating the circumstances of his death, which leads to something bigger than she ever conceived. Kudos to me for sticking with it all the way to the end? I saw a few reviews suggesting this was his magnum opus, so I thought it would be more impactful. I didn’t hate the book, but I can understand DNFing as soon as you start to question the read.

## Nonfiction

I think “Crime Fictions” is the best book I’ve read this year, and I’ve read some great books. With examples primarily from Cook County, the author explores the systematic way that wrongful convictions have helped reinforce the racist system that is U.S. policing. She delves into the techniques and reasoning used to force black kids to confess to crimes they didn’t commit. We see how often disability is used to pin the crime and close a case. And we see the monstrosity of charging a seven-year-old boy with rape and murder. Thoroughly researched and intimate in its details, this book will make you sad and make you angry. A must-read.
