## Fiction

I won an advance copy of the “No Such Thing as Monday” audiobook through StoryGraph. Steffie is a middle-aged, working-class woman in the U.K., who has been taking care of her dying father. Her older sister left home at sixteen, and Steffie doesn’t know why (or where she went). When her father dies, will see track down her estranged sister? This book is told from Steffie’s limited perspective, and builds up first with some humor, then wallops you with some emotional situations. I was really pulled into the narrative, and I will be looking at more of Hughes’ writing as a result. Definitely recommended.

Someone I trust recommended “Dèy”, and I’m glad I picked it up. Magnolia is a Haitian American real estate agent living in Miami, 2018. While looking for her daughter Zoë’s tenth birthday gift at the mall, she experiences a mass shooting event. She deals with the trauma on her own, separated from her partner and with her parents facing violence of their own in Haiti. This book packs in a lot for such a short work, from grief and gun violence in the U.S. to the deteriorating situation in Haiti. Highly recommended.

## Nonfiction

I got a (signed) copy of “The Prison Industry” when Tylek interviewed Alec Karakatsanis around the release of “Copaganda”, and its selection for bookclub spurred me on. This is a thoroughly research walkthrough of the different aspects of the prison industry in the U.S., from construction to staffing to transport to food, as so on. It breaks down how much money is involved in the different aspects of the industry, and who makes all that money at what cost to the prisoners and those of us on the outside. There are details in here that will drop your jaw. It can read a little dry at times, with a lot of statistics, but this is a powerful resource for understanding incarceration and all the reasons to do away with it.
