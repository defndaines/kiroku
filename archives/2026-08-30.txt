## Fiction

I liked the last Adeyemi book I read, and I won an advanced reader copy of “The Siren” through goodreads. Emery is starting college at Dartmouth, leaving behind a troubled past. She’s dreamed of the school for a long time. But she is easily derailed, first by a roommate infatuated with the frat party scene, then by Roux and her circle that has been dubbed “Sirens”. Everyone warns her against getting close to Roux, even her step-brother who has shown an romantic interest in Emery. I got a third of the way through and had to drop this. I wasn’t aware of the sorority thread beforehand. I could tell that Emery had something in her past, but the reveal was dumped in the middle of yet another bad decision of which Emery has many. I jumped to the ending to see there is a supernatural element, but none of that showed in the first third.

“The End of Eddy” reads like a memoir, and the author has said about 85% is nonfiction from his own life. Eddy grows up in a working-class village in northern France. His family already has a generational history of violence, and when Eddy presents as effeminate, his father pushes macho manners onto him. In school, regular violence is already there before a scandal breaks, painting an even bigger target on his back. This is a tough read, and it portrays the backward behaviors still present in society despite all the progress it can sometimes feel like we are making.

I heard about “Lost in the Garden” through a BookTuber. In England, there is now an unending summer and the dead are wandering around attacking the slow. Heather, Rachel, and Anotonia set out for Almanby, a village they’ve all known to avoid since they were kids. Heather’s boyfriend left for Almanby six months ago and never returned. Rachel has a mysterious package to deliver. And Antonia has been roped into driving because of her unrevealed crush on Heather. This is a slow read, and you will stay confused through much of it ... and likely even after you’ve finished. I’m not sure who I’d recommend it to; a low-grade horror vibe that builds and builds, with bizarre surprises you won’t see coming.

## Short Fiction

I found “Hungry Daughters of Starving Mothers” because it won the Nebula and World Fantasy Awards. Jenny, like her mother, can sense and consume the dark thoughts of those around her. She uses dating apps to find victims, but then Harvey is different—he has a darkness unlike anything she’s consumed before. And after, it will affect her relationship with her closest friend as she loses her ability to control her hunger. This is a fun, short horror story with some great twists. It’s hard to say much more without spoiling it, but I would recommend this freely.
