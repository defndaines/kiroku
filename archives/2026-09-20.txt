## Fiction

I picked up “Satantango” because Krasznahorkai won the Nobel for literature last year. Written in the ’80s, we follow a dozen or so characters over a few days, most of them falling for a scheme promising a better life. I had to look up why people liked this book: one thing was the structure, which apparently matches a tango, with twelve chapters back and forth. There are no likable characters in the story, and for me this was a slog. I could see the artistry in phrasing here and there, but it was hard for me to grasp the social and political commentary which certainly must be there, but just felt like a lot of punching down on desperate people.

## Nonfiction

“The Pacific Circuit” sits somewhere between a history of Oakland and an economic history of our post-WWII world. We see the shifts in logistics and shipping, as trade with Asia changes how goods are made and what kinds of jobs are available. We also see the U.S. military’s role in kicking all of this off. We see the increasing diversity of the city. We see the pollution and disregard for black life. The book does a great job of connecting this all together. It would make a great primer for someone trying to understand how tech and the economy have gotten into the state we are today. Recommended.
