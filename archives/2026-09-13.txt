## Fiction

I picked up “Sunburn” for book club, so I went in mostly blind. In the early ’90s in a fictional village west of Cork where everyone knows everyone else, Lucy is struggling with all the expectations for a high school girl. Everyone assumes she’ll fall in love with the boy next door, who will inherit his family farm. But Lucy is falling for someone else, and revealing that love could mean ostracization. Besides the coming of age aspect, this book also tackles one’s sense of place, the allure of one’s hometown and one’s family, even when that challenges personal acceptance. It’s a rough but recommended read, with a protagonist that doesn’t always make the best decisions.

I’m not sure why I put a hold on “Heart, Be at Peace”, perhaps back when it won the Orwell Political Fiction Prize. In 21 connected short stories set in a town near Limerick, we explore a few days in the life, along with remembrances. Jobs have disappeared; some have found jobs while others are involved in the drug trade that preys on the already down; others have been affected by a kidnapping nearly a decade ago. It takes a while to piece together the different lives, but each story connects to an overall narrative. There is a single thread of a plot that plays out across the stories, but that doesn’t really gel until the very end. This is an interesting work that I’d recommend, though the composition could be a hurdle for some readers.

## Short Fiction

I picked up “26 Monkeys, Also The Abyss” because it won a World Fantasy Award in 2009. Aimee acquired a traveling monkey act three years ago for only a dollar. Since then, she has traveled around the U.S. putting on the show, to which the finale is the disappearance of all the monkeys from an old, claw-foot bathtub. She doesn’t know where the monkeys go, only that they come back every night. This is a wonderfully quirky story, as Aimee struggles with trying to understand what happens to the monkeys, but also with her life on the road. I can see why it won awards.

## Manga

I’d forgotten that I’d put a hold on Volume 21 of “Chainsaw Man” some time ago, then the library contacted me. I caught up with the series back in June, but the gonzo nature of this story makes it hard to pick it back up every time. I was pretty nonplussed by this volume, and I don’t think I’ll continue with the story at this point.

## Graphic Novels

Volume 14 of “The Savage Sword of Conan” was one of the more forgettable issues of this run. The main Conan story, “The Exchange”, felt a little out of character from the tales that have been running lately. I didn’t hate it, but it wasn’t impressive either. The Breckinridge Elkins story at the end was just silly, but not in an endearing way.

I bought volume two of the series directly from the author recently, but wanted to read this one (from the library) beforehand. “La Llorona in the Machine” sets up the premise of the series, which is that Shel is a split from the actual Mary Shelley,surviving until today (along with Frank), and working to protect all monsters. La Llorona has disappeared, and signs point to a popular new horror video game. Several kids have already went missing while playing, always when facing the final boss. Up front, I knew this was targeted at a young audience, maxing out at middle school. The premise and art style match the audience, and the second volume won the Nebula this year. It’s a cute story and would make a good gift for a young person in your life.
