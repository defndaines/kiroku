## Fiction

I’ve been meaning to read more Marlon James, so put a hold on “The Disappearers” when it came out. The book is told from the perspective of multiple gay men who, in Jamaica 1988, are all involved in staging a play about prison and sex. Homophobia and Christian zealotry are both mainstream, and police will look the other way or join in when there is violence against homosexuals. In this context, we see their lives and the aftermath of violence that mainstream society pretends never happened. This is not an easy read, but I can see why there is already talk of literary awards. You are fully immersed in the time period and the varied experiences of the men involved.

## Short Fiction

I learned of “The Cancellation of Michael Z” when it was shortlisted for the Nommo Awards. The story is from the perspective of Z, who publishes a semipro zine of sci-fi and fantasy stories. We don’t know why people are severing their ties to Z and his magazine, but this doesn’t look like it will just blow over in a news cycle. But a story that starts off sounding like a critique of “canceling” with sympathy for the man being canceled turns into something else. I was a bit conflicted, honestly, but at the very least this is food for thought that I wasn’t expecting.
